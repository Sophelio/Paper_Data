\subsection{Conventional estimators as restricted SIR contracts}
\label{sec:nested-representation}

\paragraph{Containment.}
Restricting SIR to the representation of a conventional sparse-regression
problem---identical state coordinates $(x,y,z)$, identical cubic library
($20$ terms), identical analytic derivatives, and an identical sequentially
thresholded least-squares rule ($\lambda=0.1$)---reproduces the canonical Lorenz
recovery. Both procedures return the exact generator support with no false
positives or negatives in any of the three equations, with maximum coefficient
error $3.2\times10^{-14}$ against the true system and maximum disagreement
$3.9\times10^{-14}$ between the two procedures. Conventional sparse recovery is
therefore contained within a restricted SIR contract; this is parity by
construction and is not evidence of an advantage.

\paragraph{Representation expansion.}
We then pose a retrospective reconstruction task: recover the withheld
coordinate $z(t)$ from observations of $x$ and $y$ alone, on $48$ trajectories
of the same system split $32/8/8$ into training, validation and protected test
sets at the level of whole trajectories. All methods share one retrospective
information set $I_k=\{x_{k-2:k+2},\,y_{k-2:k+2}\}$; the raw baseline
$\mathcal{C}_0$ is exactly that stencil, so no method receives raw information
the others are denied. From $I_k$ we declare $38$ admissible candidate
coordinates---levels, rates, curvatures, algebraic products, masked quotients,
and the regularised reference-shifted and sensitivity-centered phase families---
none of which may depend on $z$, a constraint enforced and tested at the level
of the dependency graph.

Selection on training and validation data alone retained
$\mathcal{C}^{*}=\mathcal{C}_0\cup\{Q[\dot y|x],\,Q[y|x]\}$
($12$ coordinates of $38$), frozen with its provenance before any protected
evaluation. On these coordinates the sparse estimator recovers
\begin{equation}
  z = 28.000005 - 1.0000\,Q[\dot y|x] - 1.0000\,Q[y|x],
  \label{eq:recovered-z}
\end{equation}
which is the analytic identity $z=\rho-(\dot y+y)/x$ with $\rho=28$, obtained
without that identity being supplied to the search.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
This comparison is not intended to establish universal superiority over PySINDy
or neural models. PySINDy can consume expanded or custom features when supplied
with them, and here it was: given the full expanded library it attains
$1.30\times10^{-5}$, marginally better than with the selected subset. The
experiment instead tests whether conventional model classes can be represented as
restricted SIR contracts, and whether explicit search over admissible
representations provides additional task-relevant utility. The defensible
contribution is that construction, admissibility, selection and provenance were
part of the declared search, and that the selected representation is a third the
size and far better conditioned at comparable accuracy.

\paragraph{Limits.}
Under a predeclared observational-noise sweep the neural learner's advantage does
not persist: at $1\%$ noise on $x$ and $y$ the matched raw baseline
($\mathrm{RMSE}=0.757$) outperforms both $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{all}}$ ($0.847$)
and $\mathcal{C}^{*}$ ($0.817$), because differentiation and division amplify
observation noise. The sparse estimator retains a substantial benefit
($9.01\rightarrow2.21$). Representation utility on this task is therefore
demonstrated for the sparse estimator, and for the neural learner on clean data
only. Because $z$ is \emph{exactly} linear in $\mathcal{C}^{*}$, the noiseless
comparison saturates at machine precision and the noise sweep carries the
informative contrast.
