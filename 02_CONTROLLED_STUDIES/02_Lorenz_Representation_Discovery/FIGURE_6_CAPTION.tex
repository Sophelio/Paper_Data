\caption{\textbf{Conventional model classes are nested within relational
discovery.}
\textbf{a}, Problem formulation. In the conventional workflow the scientific
choices---information boundary, admissible coordinates, relation family,
utility and validation---are supplied upstream and the estimator identifies a
model within them. SIR represents those choices as a scientific object $O$ and a
discovery contract $K_q$, and searches over qualified coordinate--relation pairs
$(C,R)$; PySINDy enters as one admissible relation solver $R_q(C)$ \emph{inside}
that search rather than as an alternative to it.
\textbf{b}, Containment. Restricted to the full-state Lorenz problem with exact
derivatives, the SIR engine (executed natively through the SIR MCP) recovers the
canonical generator with exact support in all three equations
($\dot x=10y-10x$, $\dot y=27.998x-0.999y-1.000xz$, $\dot z=-2.667z+1.000xy$;
maximum coefficient deviation $\approx 2\times10^{-3}$).
\textbf{c}, One clean observational object, two contracts. Reconstructing the
withheld coordinate $z$ from $x,y$ within a common five-point retrospective
information set, the accuracy contract $q_{\mathrm{acc}}$ selects the full
admissible set $C_{\mathrm{all}}$ (38 coordinates, 32 active terms) while the
compact contract $q_{\mathrm{cmp}}$ selects a 12-coordinate representation whose
confirmation error is a factor $\sim\!2$ larger but which is four orders of
magnitude better conditioned. A conventional degree-2 polynomial fit on the same
raw stencil is shown as a strong baseline. Bars are protected-confirmation RMSE
on 24 previously unseen trajectories; the dashed line marks $\sigma_z$.
\textbf{d}, Observational uncertainty. When the scientific object explicitly
carries an observational-noise model, the robust contract selects the same
coordinates as the accuracy contract: uncertainty changed the \emph{qualification}
of the representation, not the representation itself, and all representations
degrade sharply. Statistical unit is the trajectory throughout.}
